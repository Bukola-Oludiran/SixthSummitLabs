# Logo principles that change the design

Use these as questions to resolve a particular identity, not a mandatory minimalist aesthetic.

## Meaning and distinctiveness

- Identify the business-specific idea before selecting shapes. A landscape business need not have a
  mountain, a technology company need not have circuit lines, and a carpenter need not have crossed
  tools. A familiar motif can work when its construction has a particular reason.
- Prefer one strong relationship over many competing symbols. If the explanation needs a long list
  of metaphors, simplify the concept or choose the most relevant one.
- Compare alternatives at equal size in one colour. Differences should be in the idea or silhouette,
  rather than the same generic mark with another palette. Do not claim global uniqueness or trademark
  clearance from a design exercise.

## Silhouette, counters and figure/ground

- Squint or reduce the mark. The outer shape should still have a readable organization; decoration
  should not be holding the composition together.
- Check the negative spaces as deliberately as the solid shapes. Narrow valleys, small counters and
  acute notches may close at small sizes even when their coordinates are mathematically consistent.
- Use true holes or compound paths for cutouts. View the logo on both light and dark surfaces so a
  white cover shape cannot masquerade as transparent negative space.
- A symbol's centre of mass can differ from its bounding-box centre. Balance the visible weight;
  circular/pointed forms may need overshoot, and a compact icon can need extra height beside type.
- Match the character of corners, joins, stroke weight and curves. Use exceptions intentionally;
  avoid accidental mixtures caused by unrelated primitive defaults.

## Wordmarks and signatures

- Start with the exact business name and intended capitalization. Check every glyph in the final
  typeface; a missing glyph or fallback font changes the identity.
- Inspect pair spacing by eye. Uniform tracking is a starting point, not a substitute for kerning.
  Look at troublesome pairs, wide capitals, punctuation, numerals and the gap between symbol and name.
- Choose type for the business's voice and the shapes of its actual name. A custom letterform must
  improve recognition or character, not merely make the text harder to read.
- Balance perceived size, weight and baseline. A mark replacing a numeral may need to be physically
  taller than the neighbouring capitals to appear equally prominent. Retest at the header's actual
  width after enlarging it; do not rely only on an oversized presentation.
- Let descriptors be subordinate without making them unreadable. A compact signature may omit an
  optional descriptor when that use is agreed; do not silently drop part of the legal/business name.

## Colour and production

- Use observed brand colours when preservation is part of the brief. Explain proposed colours as
  proposals; do not present palette inspiration as evidence from the business.
- Colour can help recognition, but inspect a one-colour solution too. A flat print, vinyl cut,
  embroidery, favicon and dark header impose different limits. Pick the checks that match the uses.
- A reversed mark may need an optical adjustment to avoid appearing heavier or closing its counters.
  Supply that as a named variant, not an undocumented distortion in CSS.
- Inspect transparent output on actual backgrounds, not only a checkerboard. A raster PNG is a useful
  preview; an SVG containing an embedded PNG is not a scalable vector production file.
- Keep live type in an editable master and outline the production SVG. Record the exact font family
  and style; do not assume outlining grants permission to redistribute the original font file.

## Proportionate review

Inspect the mark in one colour, the name at normal reading size, the smallest intended application,
and a reversed treatment before using mockups to judge it. Record specific observations, such as
“the inner notch closes below 24px,” rather than assigning a decorative quality score. If there is
no reliable visual evidence, report the missing check instead of calling the identity fully tested.
