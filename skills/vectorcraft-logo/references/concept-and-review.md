# Develop a concept, then test what was drawn

Read for a new identity or substantial redesign. For a spacing/size correction, use only the
relevant typography and application checks. The process synthesises the linked research; its
diagnostic prompts are practical heuristics, not validated audience tests.

## Make the brief useful

Keep a short working note with the exact name, audience, desired character, evidence from the
business, assets to retain and the applications that matter. Distinguish facts from proposals.
Turn vague adjectives into useful decisions: what visible treatment would make this particular
business feel welcoming, precise or exuberant? Do not fabricate a heritage story to justify a shape.

When designing a new identity, inspect relevant competitors and adjacent visual references where
available. Record source links and recurring conventions. Use them to identify confusion risks
and room for distinction; do not build a collage of borrowed logo features. A visual similarity
check is not trademark clearance. If research is unavailable, acknowledge that limit.

## Give the alternatives a real choice

Describe promising territories in plain words, then make quick visual studies. A business-specific
lettering route, an abstract relationship and an expressive emblem are examples of different
territories, not a compulsory set. Develop alternatives until their ideas can be meaningfully
compared; do not pad the set with weaker recolours or produce a full kit for every sketch.

For each plausible direction, note its intended reading, the formal feature that distinguishes it,
its connection to the brief and its main risk. If its only rationale is that the category commonly
uses that symbol, investigate a stronger construction or another route. Familiar motifs can still
work; do not reject a requested motif just for being familiar.

Compare the strongest directions at similar perceived size and presentation quality. A one-colour
view helps compare silhouette; also inspect colour when it is integral to the idea. Select using
the brief, recognition cues, legibility and practical uses. Do not let a more elaborate mockup
decide the winner. Keep a concise reason for selection and the alternative's tradeoff.

## Refine type and geometry together

Set the exact business name in plausible type directions. Compare its actual letter shapes,
proportions and rhythm; a font catalogue specimen is insufficient. A wordmark-only solution can
be complete. Customise only where it gives the name useful character without breaking its reading.

Inspect counters, joins, terminals and adjacent negative spaces. Correct optical weight and
alignment before adding decorative effects. For kerning, inspect the problematic pair, then the
whole word: fixing one gap may produce a new break elsewhere. Do not force equal numeric gaps or
geometric proportions at the expense of visible balance. Keep the live-type source and, for custom
lettering, the editable paths and original type reference where applicable.

When a symbol replaces a letter or numeral, check the name before explaining the symbolism. If the
reading becomes ambiguous, revise it or use the symbol beside a readable wordmark. For a refinement
request, preserve the approved idea and make the smallest change that resolves the visible defect.

## Critique with evidence

Look at actual renders before writing the rationale. For each material issue, record **observation
→ consequence → revision → recheck**, with the file/application and displayed size. For example:
“The inner valley closes at 24px on dark; the peaks merge; widen the valley in a labelled compact
variant; re-render at 24px and inspect.” A flattering score cannot replace that evidence.

Use the relevant lenses:

| Lens | What to inspect | What calls for revision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Identity | Connection to the brief and recognisable formal feature | A concept interchangeable with nearby competitors or justified only by a long story |
| Name | Correct spelling, reading order and glyphs | A replaced letter reads as another character; cramped or scattered pairs split the name |
| Shape | Silhouette, counters, weight and curve consistency | Accidental tangencies, pinched gaps, weak joins or detail that vanishes in the intended use |
| Versions | Colour, genuine monochrome, reversed and any compact form | A false background hole, lost feature or undocumented change of identity |
| Application | Actual header/signature/other agreed use at its real size | It fits only after excessive padding, competes with navigation or needs unsafe enlargement |
| Production | Reopened master against outlined export and PNG | Missing glyphs, altered spacing, clipped geometry or lost transparency |

Preserve intentional asymmetry, unusual type or illustration when it works. Do not manufacture
defects to justify a review. Distinguish “I find this familiar” from demonstrated competitor
confusion, and distinguish a model's readability judgment from observed human recognition.

Recheck changed areas and their dependent applications. Stop when material defects are resolved
and the brief is met; report any unresolved or untested requirement. Do not loop indefinitely to
chase a numeric quality score.

## Record completion honestly

Keep a short review note beside the working artifacts: selected direction and tradeoff; rendered
files and sizes inspected; application evidence; revisions and rechecks; observed minimum use;
remaining limits. A task may be delivered with a disclosed limitation, but never label an unperformed
check as passed. `proof.py` supplies structural findings and pending visual checks. It cannot
certify brand quality, verify a real monochrome treatment or approve the identity for the client.
